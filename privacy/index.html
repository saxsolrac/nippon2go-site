Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 26, 2026

The short version

  • You don't need an account to use Nippon2Go. Without one, your trip, routes and checklist are stored only on your iPhone and we cannot see them.
  • If you sign in with Apple or Google, we create a Nippon2Go account on our server in Madrid (Google Cloud). It keeps an identifier from Apple or Google and your first name, never your email address.
  • With an account you can save a copy of your trip and share a trip with up to seven companions. Only then is that trip stored on our server, and everyone in a shared trip sees it, with the first names of the people in it.
  • Our place server passes Google place and hotel requests on to Google, revokes Sign in with Apple when you delete such an account and counts visits to our website’s download pages. It doesn’t keep your trips, your hotel searches or any Apple codes or tokens.
  • The app has no ads and no tracking, and we never sell your data. Only if you agree, it sends anonymous usage statistics to PostHog, on servers in the European Union, so we know what to improve; you can change your answer at any time in Profile → Privacy. Our website loads a script from Travelpayouts, our affiliate network, and its download pages count visits without cookies; the one at nippon2go.com/app/ also uses Google Analytics, only if you accept (see section 6).
  • You can delete your account at any time in the app, or on our website for a Google account. That deletes your account and your copy from our server and takes you out of every shared trip.
  • Some features use Apple, Google and Wikimedia services, which handle data under their own privacy policies.

1. Who we are

This policy applies to the Nippon2Go iPhone app ("Nippon2Go", "we", "us") and to the Nippon2Go account it lets you create. We are responsible for the personal data described here. If you have any questions about your privacy, contact us at carlos@nippon2go.com.

2. Data stored on your device

Nippon2Go saves the following on your iPhone, in the app's own storage:

  • Your trip details: dates (or just the month, if you don’t know them yet), daily plans, routes, saved places, flights, and visit times and durations.
  • Your packing checklist, including any items you add.
  • Your preferences, such as app language and route pace.
  • A record of which plans you have added to your calendar, so they aren't added twice.
  • Whether you want trip reminders, and the reminders your iPhone has scheduled (see section 7).
  • Your answer to the usage statistics question and, if you agree to them, the random identifier that goes with them and any statistics not sent yet (see section 6).
  • If you are signed in, the key of your session with our server, in your iPhone's secure Keychain (see section 3).
  • If you are in a shared trip, the group's version of the trip and any of your changes not sent yet, so you can use it without a connection (see section 5).

This data is not sent to us or to anyone else, except what you choose to save or share with your account (sections 4 and 5) and the details described in section 6 (such as the Google identifier of a place or hotel saved in your trip, or your city and dates in a booking link you open). It is removed when you delete the app.

3. Your Nippon2Go account

Signing in is optional. Without an account the app works the same, except saving a copy of your trip and sharing a trip, which need one.

What our server keeps

When you sign in, the app sends our account server a token from Apple or Google that proves who you are. The server checks it with Apple’s or Google’s public keys and then keeps:

  • A random account number created by our server.
  • Whether you signed in with Apple or Google, and the account identifier that Apple or Google gives Nippon2Go for you, so we recognise you the next time you sign in.
  • Your first name, taken from Apple or Google when you sign in, or the one you choose in Profile → Account → Name for the group. People in your shared trips see it (section 5).
  • When your account was created and last updated.
  • Your sessions: the app receives a random session key and keeps it in the Keychain, and the server stores only a fingerprint of it (a one-way hash), never the key itself. A session ends after 90 days without use, and its record is then deleted.

The server doesn’t keep your email address, the Apple or Google token or your photo.

Sign in with Apple

If you sign in with Apple, Apple gives the app a user identifier, a signed token and, the first time only, your name and email address. You can choose to hide your email. The app sends the token to our server together with a one-time random value that protects it from being reused, and your first name; it never sends your email. The app also keeps your name and email in your iPhone's secure Keychain to show that you are signed in. Because Apple shares them only once, the app keeps them there after you sign out, so it can show them again the next time you sign in with Apple. Like other Keychain data, they can stay on your iPhone after you delete the app, where only Nippon2Go can read them, unless you delete your account first (see section 4).

Sign in with Google

If you sign in with Google, Google's sign-in service gives the app your basic profile (name and email address) and a signed token. The app sends the token to our server, which takes from it your Google account identifier and your first name; it doesn’t keep your email. Google's software keeps your sign-in details in your iPhone's Keychain. We don't ask for access to any other Google services, such as Calendar or Drive. Google processes your sign-in under the Google Privacy Policy.

4. A copy of your trip, and deleting your account

A copy of your trip

In Profile → Account you can save a copy of your trip to your account and get it back on another iPhone or Android phone. The copy contains your trip (its name, dates, days and cities, plans with their notes, and your flights), your answer to “When are you going to Japan?”, your favourites, My routes (with the hotels you chose), your own checklist, the places you added and which plans you marked as visited, plus the app’s version. Photos, settings, reminders and statistics are never in it. Our server keeps one copy per account, compressed, and replaces it each time you save. When you join a shared trip, the app first saves your own trip there, so you can get it back later, unless it is still the sample trip.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account at any time in Profile → Account → Delete account. If you signed in with Google, you can also do it on our website at nippon2go.com/delete-account/, signing in with the same Google account. Our server then deletes straight away your account, the link to your Apple or Google identifier, your first name, your copy and the invitations you created and the phones registered for change alerts, and your sessions stop working (their records are removed a day later). The technical records of the changes you sent to shared trips, which contain only your random account number, are removed within 30 days.

You also leave every shared trip. What you added stays in the trip for the others, but your name is removed from it: it shows no author, and your ticks on the group checklist are removed. If you organised a trip, the person who has been in it longest becomes the organiser; if nobody else is left, the trip is deleted from our server.

If you signed in with Apple, Apple asks you to confirm and gives the app a one-time authorization code. The app sends that code to our place server, which uses it with Apple to revoke Nippon2Go’s access to your Apple ID straight away. Apple’s reply to the server contains your Apple user identifier (and may contain your email address); the server uses it only to revoke the access and doesn’t keep or log the code, the tokens or anything in them. If you signed in with Google, the app asks Google to revoke its access. In both cases the app then removes everything it kept for the account on your iPhone, including the name and email kept in the Keychain. Your trips stay on your iPhone until you delete the app.

5. Shared trips

With an account, you can share your trip with up to seven people, or join someone else’s. Everyone in it sees the same trip on their own phone, iPhone or Android, and can change it unless they only have permission to view it.

What is shared

  • The trip: its name, dates (or month or number of days), days and cities, plans (time, duration, type, note and place) and flights (airports, including the home airport used to search for them, flight numbers, times and number of adults). A place is either one from our catalogue, a hotel saved by its Google identifier and city only, or a place someone added, with its name, city and map position.
  • The group checklist, with who takes care of each task and who ticked it.
  • For each of these, who added or last changed it and when.
  • The people in the trip: their first name, their permission (organiser, can edit or can view) and when they joined.

Everyone in the trip sees all of this, including the first names of the others and who added or changed each thing. Your favourites, My routes, your own checklist, which plans you marked as visited, photos, settings, reminders and statistics stay personal and are never shared.

Invitations

The organiser invites people with a link or a code that works for 14 days and can be cancelled before then; it also stops working if the person who created it leaves the trip. Anyone who has the code can see, before joining, the trip’s name, its dates or month and number of days, its cities, how many people are in it and the organiser’s first name, so share it only with the people you want to invite. A trip can have up to eight people.

Leaving, being removed and deleted items

You can leave a trip at any time, and the organiser can remove people or change their permission. Whoever leaves or is removed keeps the trip on their phone as their own; it stays on our server for the others. What they added stays in the trip, linked only to their random account number, and the apps no longer show their name; the tasks they were taking care of are left without anyone. When the last person leaves, the trip is deleted from our server. When someone deletes a plan or a task, our server keeps only a marker so the other phones remove it too: for a plan, its day, time, duration and type and who deleted it, without its name, note or place; for a task, its category and who deleted it, without its title. To avoid applying the same changes twice, the server keeps a technical record of each batch of changes it received for 30 days.

Change alerts

While you are in a shared trip, the app can tell you when someone else changes it: Profile → Reminders → Group changes, on by default, once you allow notifications. For this, the app sends our account server Apple’s notification token for this iPhone, whether it is a test or an App Store build, and a random installation number the app makes once on this iPhone and keeps in its Keychain. The server keeps them with your account number and deletes them when you turn the switch off, sign out, leave the trip or delete your account, when Apple says the token is no longer valid, or after 60 days without the app sending them again. About two minutes after someone else changes the trip, the server asks Apple’s push notification service to deliver an alert that carries only the trip’s number and a change number: no names, places or anything else from the trip. To wait those two minutes, the server uses Google Cloud Tasks in Belgium, which holds only the trip’s number. On your iPhone, a part of the app then gets the changes from our account server and writes the alert’s text in the app’s language; it saves nothing. Apple delivers the alert under its own privacy policy.

Where it is stored

Accounts, copies and shared trips are stored by our account server, which runs on Google Cloud (Cloud Run and Firestore) in the Madrid, Spain region. Google Cloud (Google Cloud EMEA Limited, Ireland) processes this data for us as a service provider, under its Cloud Data Processing Addendum. Only our server reads and writes the database; the apps never access it directly.

6. Services that connect to the internet

Our account server

The app connects to our account server to sign you in, save or get back your copy, and send and receive the changes of a shared trip (when you open the app, every 30 seconds on My trip while a trip is shared, and when you pull down to refresh). Google Cloud keeps standard technical logs of the requests our server receives (the time, your IP address, your device type, the address requested, which can include a trip number or an invitation code, and the result) for up to 30 days, for security and to keep the service running. To stop abuse, the server limits how many requests each IP address and each account can make; it keeps these counts only in memory, for one minute.

Google Places (place photos, details and hotel search)

To show place photos, the details of the hotel saved in your trip and hotel search results, the app sends requests to our place server, also hosted on Google Cloud in Madrid, which passes them on to Google Maps Platform. These include the Google identifier of the place, or the hotel name you type and the city, plus your chosen language. The server doesn’t keep your trips or hotel searches. Google Cloud keeps standard technical logs of the requests our server receives (the time, your IP address, your device type and which Google place was requested) for up to 30 days, for security and to keep the service running. When the app starts, it also gets from our server the key that Google’s place details card needs, and keeps it only in memory. The place details card, the photos and their authors’ pictures are then loaded directly from Google, so Google also receives technical information such as your IP address and device type. Google may collect information about how its software is used. This is described in the Google Privacy Policy. Photos and place details are shown with Google's attributions and are not stored by the app.

Wikimedia Commons (some place photos)

When a place has no Google photo, or Google’s photo can’t be loaded, the app may show a photo loaded directly from Wikimedia Commons, so the Wikimedia Foundation receives technical information such as your IP address and device type. This is covered by the Wikimedia Foundation Privacy Policy.

Account deletion with Apple

When you delete an account created with Sign in with Apple, the app sends Apple’s one-time authorization code to our place server, which passes it to Apple to revoke the app’s access (see section 4). Google Cloud keeps the same standard technical logs of that request (the time, your IP address and your device type) for up to 30 days.

Account deletion on our website

The page nippon2go.com/delete-account/ uses Google’s sign-in for websites, so your browser loads Google’s sign-in script and Google receives technical information such as your IP address and may use its own cookies to sign you in. The page sends Google’s token to our account server, keeps the session it gets back only in the page’s memory and forgets it when the account is deleted or you close the page. If you didn’t have an account, the sign-in creates one for a moment and the page deletes it straight away. Google handles this under the Google Privacy Policy.

Apple Maps

Maps in the app are provided by Apple and are covered by Apple's Privacy Policy.

Links to other services

Some buttons open other websites or apps, such as Google Maps (directions), Booking.com (hotels), Klook (activities and JR Pass), Airalo (eSIM), Skyscanner (flights), IATI (travel insurance), Preply (Japanese lessons) and Visit Japan Web (Japan’s entry forms). Some links include details needed for your search, such as the destination, the city or your trip dates. Some of these are affiliate or referral links: Booking.com goes through the CJ affiliate network, Skyscanner, Airalo and Preply through Impact, and Klook and IATI through their own programmes. When you tap one, that network or provider records the click so that, if you book, we may receive a small commission at no extra cost to you. Once you leave Nippon2Go, the other service's terms and privacy policy apply. We only receive aggregated reports of clicks and bookings, never information that identifies you or what you do there.

Anonymous usage statistics (PostHog, only if you agree)

From version 1.12, the first time you open the app it asks whether you want to share anonymous usage statistics to help us see what to improve. If you say no, the app sends nothing and doesn’t even start the service. You can change your answer at any time in Profile → Privacy → Anonymous statistics.

If you agree, the app sends PostHog (PostHog Inc.), which processes them on our behalf on its servers in the European Union (Frankfurt, Germany), notes of what you do in it: when you open it and where you are in your trip (before, during or after it, and how far away it is, in ranges), which screens you visit, which catalog places you open or add to your routes, which kind of link you tap (for example, “Klook tickets”, never the address), when you add a suggested route to your itinerary, how you answer “When are you going to Japan?” (exact dates, just the month or not known yet), whether you accept, decline or turn off trip reminders (or get them quietly) and which reminder you tap (for example, “the hotels one”), when a nippon2go.com link opens the app, which screen and city it opens (for example, “the Kyoto route”), in the welcome steps of a new install which steps you see, whether you finish or skip them (and at which step) and, when you finish, the catalog cities you choose and how many days the trip lasts, and, when you save your trip or flights, only the trip’s length in days, its start month, the number of flight legs and the airport in Japan. For shared trips, it notes when you share a trip, create an invitation, open one (from a link or with a code), join a trip, change someone’s permission, remove someone or leave a trip, with the permission involved (edit or view) but never names, codes or the trip’s content. When you sign in with Apple or Google, it notes whether it worked, you cancelled it or it failed, without saying which one or your account. When you plan your trip, it notes which day “My trip” shows you (before, during or after the trip, or without dates), whether you open the “What to do now” task or mark it with “I already have it” and which task it is (for example, “the eSIM”), what you change in a plan (the time, duration, booked entry, note, order or day, but never what it says or the time you chose), whether you move a faraway plan to another day or keep it where it is when the app suggests a day trip and, when you add a place to a day from “Explore”, whether you pick the day it suggests. Each note also carries the app’s language and version, and PostHog’s software adds technical details about your phone: the iPhone model, the iOS version, the screen size, the phone’s language and time zone, and whether it’s on Wi‑Fi or mobile data.

We never send your name, your email, your account, what you type (such as your trip’s name, your notes or your hotel searches), your trip’s exact dates or your location. The statistics go with a random identifier that the app creates on your iPhone, unrelated to your account or to Apple or Google, and no profiles of people are built. Like any internet server, PostHog receives your phone’s IP address when the data arrives, but it is set up to discard it without storing it, and the app asks it not to use it to work out your location. The app doesn’t record your screen or log your taps, and we don’t use these statistics for advertising or combine them with data from other companies. Only we see the results, in PostHog’s dashboard, and PostHog keeps the statistics for one year before deleting them.

If you turn the statistics off, the app stops sending them and deletes from your iPhone the random identifier and any statistics not yet sent; if you turn them on again, it starts with a new identifier.

Travelpayouts (this website only, not the app)

The nippon2go.com website loads a script from Travelpayouts, the affiliate network we work with, to confirm that we own the site and to manage affiliate links. When you open a page, your browser downloads the script from Travelpayouts’ servers, which receive technical data such as your IP address, your browser and the page you are visiting. The script can turn some links to travel services into affiliate links, so that Travelpayouts can record the click and, if you book, pay us a small commission at no extra cost to you. Travelpayouts may use cookies or similar technologies to do this. It handles this data under its own privacy policy. The Nippon2Go app does not include this script.

Visit counter and Google Analytics (this website’s download pages only, not the app)

On the download pages (nippon2go.com/app/ in each of its seven languages, and nippon2go.com/go/, the link in our ads), our place server counts each visit and each tap on the App Store and Google Play buttons. This uses no cookies and saves nothing on your device: your browser sends only which page it is (the language of /app/, or /go/), what happened (a visit, or which button was tapped) and, on /go/, the code of the link you came from (for example, the one in our Instagram bio or in an ad). Google Cloud keeps the same standard technical logs of that request (the time, your IP address and your device type) for up to 30 days. We only look at the totals for each page and each link.

If you accept in the cookie notice on nippon2go.com/app/, it also loads Google Analytics, which uses cookies to show us how many people visit, roughly where from and on which kind of device, and how many tap a store button. Google signals and advertising features are turned off, and Google Analytics doesn’t log or store IP addresses. If you reject, Google Analytics isn’t loaded at all. You can change your answer at any time with the “Cookies” link at the bottom of that page; your answer is saved in your browser for a year. Google handles this data under the Google Privacy Policy (see also how Google uses information from sites that use its services). The Nippon2Go app doesn’t use Google Analytics.

7. Permissions

  • Calendar: only when you choose to add your itinerary to your calendar, Nippon2Go asks for permission to add events. It cannot read, change or delete your existing events.
  • Notifications: your own iPhone schedules trip reminders (what to book before you go, your plan every morning in Japan and one when you’re back) from your trip details: they don’t go through any server and no data is sent for them. From version 2.6, until you answer, the app uses iOS’s quiet (“provisional”) notifications, which don’t ask you first: they arrive without sound, only in Notification Center, where you can keep them or turn them off. When you save your trip’s dates or month, Nippon2Go asks whether you want them on screen and with sound and, only if you say yes, asks for permission to do so. You can turn them off in Profile → Reminders → Trip reminders. The alerts about changes to a shared trip come from our server through Apple and have their own switch, Profile → Reminders → Group changes (section 5).
  • Location: Nippon2Go does not access your device's location.
  • Pasteboard: to join a shared trip you can paste an invitation with iOS’s Paste button. The app reads only what you paste with it.

You can change permissions at any time in your iPhone's Settings.

8. What we don't do

  • The app doesn't use advertising or tracking tools, and it only sends anonymous usage statistics to PostHog if you agree (section 6). Our website only loads the Travelpayouts script, Google’s sign-in on the account deletion page and, on the download pages, the visit counter and (on /app/, if you accept) Google Analytics described in section 6.
  • The app doesn't track you across other companies' apps or websites.
  • We don't sell, rent or share your personal data. Your shared trips are seen only by the people in them.
  • Our account server keeps only your account, your copy and your shared trips (sections 3 to 5). Our place server gives the app the key for Google’s place cards, passes Google place and hotel requests on to Google, asks Apple to revoke the app’s access when you delete an account created with Sign in with Apple and counts visits to our website’s download pages; it doesn’t keep your trips, your hotel searches or any Apple codes or tokens.

9. When you contact us

If you email us, we receive your email address and whatever you include in your message. We use it only to reply and help you, and we delete it once it is no longer needed for that purpose. We do not add you to any mailing list.

10. Children

Nippon2Go is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from children.

11. Your rights

The trip on your iPhone is always under your control: you can edit it in the app or remove it by deleting the app. Your account, the link to your Apple or Google account and your copy are kept until you delete your account, even if you don’t use the app for a long time: we don’t delete them for inactivity. You can see and change your first name in Profile → Account → Name for the group, get your copy back in Profile → Account, and leave any shared trip. To sign out, go to Profile → Account → Sign out. If you used Sign in with Apple, your name and email stay in your iPhone's Keychain after signing out (see section 3). To delete your account and everything our server and the app kept for it, go to Profile → Account → Delete account (the option appears while you are signed in; if you have signed out, sign in again first) or, for a Google account, use nippon2go.com/delete-account/. To stop sending usage statistics, turn off Profile → Privacy → Anonymous statistics: the app deletes the random identifier and any statistics not yet sent. Because the ones already sent aren’t linked to your name or email, we can’t tell which are yours.

We use your account, your copy and your shared trips to provide the features you ask for; the technical logs and request limits to keep the service secure, which is our legitimate interest; and the usage statistics only with your consent, which you can withdraw at any time. You can ask us for a copy of the data our server keeps about you, or to correct or delete it, by writing to carlos@nippon2go.com; because we don’t keep your email address, we may ask you to confirm the request from the app. You can do the same for any correspondence you have sent us. Depending on where you live, you may also have the right to complain to your local data protection authority.

12. Changes to this policy

If we change how Nippon2Go handles data, we will update this page and the date at the top.
