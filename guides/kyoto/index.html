Things to do in Kyoto

Kyoto was Japan's imperial capital for more than a thousand years and still has hundreds of temples and shrines, wooden townhouse streets in Higashiyama and Gion, and bamboo groves out west in Arashiyama.

By Nippon2Go · Updated 25 November 2026

Distances are short, but the famous places fill up early, so the order of your visits matters more here than anywhere else. Each day below groups what is close together, the way Nippon2Go plans your route.

How many days?

3 days for the essentials and 5 to take it slowly. The first three days below cover Fushimi Inari and Higashiyama, Arashiyama with the Golden Pavilion, and Gion; days four and five add Heian Jingū, Sanjūsangen-dō, Tō-ji and a tea ceremony.

Nippon2Go splits your places into days at your pace: Relaxed (about 5 hours of visits and travel a day), Balanced (7) or Active (9). Visit times are the app's estimates.

Kyoto day by day

Day 1 · Fushimi Inari and Higashiyama

  • Fushimi Inari Taisha

    Thousands of red torii gates form one of Japan’s most iconic trails.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Kiyomizu-dera

    A historic temple overlooking the narrow streets of Higashiyama.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Sannenzaka

    A street in Higashiyama with shops and traditional buildings, near Kiyomizu-dera.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h

Day 2 · Arashiyama and the Golden Pavilion

  • Arashiyama Bamboo Grove

    A path among tall bamboo in Arashiyama, west of Kyoto.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)

    The Golden Pavilion, covered in gold leaf and mirrored in its pond.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

Day 3 · Nishiki, Gion and Shirakawa

  • Nishiki Market

    Local food stops for sampling sweets, fish and pickles.

    Restaurant · Visit: about 1 h

  • Gion

    A neighbourhood in Higashiyama for strolling through the streets and discovering Kyoto’s traditional atmosphere.

    Activity · Visit: about 2 h

  • Shirakawa Canal

    A walk beside the Shirakawa Canal in the Gion area.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

Day 4 · Heian Jingū and Sanjūsangen-dō

  • Heian Jingū

    Shrine from 1895 with a giant red torii and gardens behind the main hall.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Sanjūsangen-dō

    Temple with 1,001 statues of Kannon in a wooden hall about 120 metres long.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

Day 5 · Tō-ji and a tea ceremony

  • Tō-ji

    A Buddhist temple with a five-storey pagoda, southwest of Kyoto Station.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Tea ceremony workshop

    An intimate introduction to matcha and the traditional tea ceremony.

    Activity · Visit: about 2 h

More places in Kyoto in the app

  • Yasaka Pagoda (Hōkan-ji)

    The pagoda of Hōkan-ji Temple, among the streets of Higashiyama.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Kura Sushi Plus Kyoto

    A conveyor-belt sushi restaurant in central Kyoto.

    Restaurant · Visit: about 1 h

Tips

  • Fushimi Inari is open around the clock and free: early in the morning it is almost empty.
  • Kiyomizu-dera, Sannenzaka and Yasaka Pagoda make one easy morning on foot.
  • Some lanes in Gion are private and closed to visitors and photos: follow the signs.

Getting there

  • Tokyo → Kyoto: Shinkansen, about 2 h 15 min · about 14,000 yen
  • Kyoto → Osaka: JR train, about 30 min · about 600 yen
  • Kyoto → Nara: Train, about 45 min · about 750 yen
  • Kyoto → Hiroshima: Shinkansen, about 1 h 45 min · about 11,900 yen

Usual timetable times and fares for one adult, one way (reserved seat on the shinkansen), rounded. Check them before you travel.
