Kamakura in one day

Kamakura was the seat of Japan's first shogunate in the late 12th century and is now the favourite day trip from Tokyo: the open-air bronze Great Buddha, temples among the hills, Komachi Street and beaches looking out to Enoshima.

By Nippon2Go · Updated 25 November 2026

On clear days you can also see Mt Fuji from the coast.

How many days?

One day. It is about 60 minutes by JR train from Tokyo and about 25 from Yokohama.

Kamakura day by day

Day 1 · Great Buddha, Hase-dera, Hachiman-gū and Komachi

  • Kōtoku-in (Great Buddha of Kamakura)

    The temple of the Great Buddha of Kamakura, an open-air bronze statue over 11 metres tall.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Hase-dera

    A hillside temple with gardens, caves and views over Sagami Bay.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Tsurugaoka Hachiman-gū

    Kamakura’s main Shinto shrine, at the end of Wakamiya-ōji Avenue.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Komachi Street

    A pedestrian shopping street by the station, with shops and food stalls on the way to Tsurugaoka Hachiman-gū.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h

More places in Kamakura in the app

  • Cape Inamuragasaki

    A seaside cape with views of Enoshima and, on clear days, Mount Fuji.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Shichirigahama Beach

    A beach on Sagami Bay with views of Enoshima and Mount Fuji.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Hōkoku-ji

    A Zen temple famous for its bamboo grove and small teahouse.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h

  • Kamakura-Kōkōmae Station

    A seaside Enoden station, known for its railway crossing overlooking the bay.

    Sight

  • Kamakura Museum of History and Culture

    A museum on Kamakura’s history in a modern building surrounded by gardens.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Zeniarai Benten Shrine

    A shrine in a cave where visitors wash money in spring water to bring prosperity.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Sasuke Inari Shrine

    A forest shrine with paths of red torii gates and fox figures.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Sugimoto-dera

    Kamakura’s oldest temple, with a moss-covered stone staircase.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

Tips

  • The Great Buddha and Hase-dera are both in Hase, a short walk apart.
  • The Enoden train runs along the coast to Enoshima; Kamakura-Kōkōmae is the stop with the famous seaside level crossing.
  • It gets crowded at weekends: go on a weekday if you can.

Getting there

  • Tokyo → Kamakura: JR train, about 1 h · about 1,050 yen
  • Yokohama → Kamakura: JR train, about 25 min · about 440 yen
