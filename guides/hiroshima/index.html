Things to do in Hiroshima and Miyajima

Hiroshima is visited for its history: the Peace Memorial Park, the Atomic Bomb Dome and the Peace Memorial Museum tell what happened on 6 August 1945, with great care and no sensationalism.

By Nippon2Go · Updated 25 November 2026

About 50 minutes away by train and ferry is Miyajima, the island of the great torii that seems to float at high tide, with Itsukushima Shrine and Mt Misen.

How many days do you need in Hiroshima?

Hiroshima: 2 days: one for the city and Peace Park, and one for Miyajima. By shinkansen it is about 85 minutes from Osaka and about 105 from Kyoto.

Nippon2Go splits your places into days at your pace: Relaxed (about 5 hours of visits and travel a day), Balanced (7) or Active (9). Visit times are the app's estimates.

Hiroshima day by day

Day 1 · Peace Park and Shukkei-en

  • Atomic Bomb Dome (Genbaku Dome)

    The ruins of a 1915 exhibition hall that remained standing about 160 metres from the hypocentre; a World Heritage Site since 1996.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum

    A museum documenting the atomic bombing through victims’ belongings, photographs and documents; booking tickets online is recommended.

    Activity · Visit: about 2 h

  • Shukkei-en Garden

    A garden created in 1620 for the Asano lords, with a central pond, islets and bridges that recreate landscapes in miniature.

    Activity · Visit: about 1 h

Day 2 · Miyajima

  • Miyajima Island (Itsukushima)

    The island of Itsukushima Shrine, about 10 minutes by ferry from Miyajimaguchi (with a 100-yen visitor tax).

    Sight

  • Itsukushima Shrine

    A World Heritage Shinto shrine whose halls, built over the sea, seem to float at high tide.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Mount Misen Observatory

    A viewpoint on the 535-metre summit of Mount Misen, with 360° views over the islands of the Seto Inland Sea.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

More places in Hiroshima in the app

  • Children’s Peace Monument

    A 1958 monument dedicated to Sadako Sasaki and the child victims of the bomb, surrounded by paper cranes sent from all over the world.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Hiroshima Orizuru Tower

    A building next to the Atomic Bomb Dome with a wooden rooftop observation deck and a wall where you can drop paper cranes.

    Activity · Visit: about 2 h

  • Hiroshima Castle Ninomaru

    The rebuilt wooden gate and turrets of Hiroshima Castle, free to enter; the main keep has been closed since March 2026.

    Sight · Visit: about 2 h

  • Hiroshima Gokoku Shrine

    A Shinto shrine in the grounds of Hiroshima Castle, rebuilt in 1956 and visited every year by the Carp baseball team.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Ōtorii (Grand Torii of Itsukushima)

    A vermilion torii gate about 16 metres tall standing in the sea, which seems to float at high tide and can be reached on foot at low tide.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Daishō-in

    A Shingon temple, the oldest on Miyajima, with prayer wheels along its stairs, rakan statues and the Henjōkutsu cave.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Momijidani Park

    A park in a valley at the foot of Mount Misen, with about 700 maples that turn red in November and the Momiji-bashi bridge.

    Sight · Visit: about 1 h 30 min

  • Miyajima Ropeway

    A two-section ropeway between Momijidani Park and Shishiiwa Station, about a 30-minute walk from the summit of Mount Misen.

    Activity · Visit: about 2 h

Tips

  • Book your Peace Memorial Museum ticket online.
  • Check the tide table: at high tide the torii seems to float, at low tide you can walk up to it.
  • Mt Misen is a two-stage ropeway ride and then about 30 minutes on foot.

Getting there

  • Osaka → Hiroshima: Shinkansen, about 1 h 25 min · about 11,000 yen
  • Kyoto → Hiroshima: Shinkansen, about 1 h 45 min · about 11,900 yen
  • Tokyo → Hiroshima: Shinkansen, about 4 h · about 19,700 yen
  • Hiroshima → Miyajima: JR train and ferry, about 50 min · about 720 yen

Usual timetable times and fares for one adult, one way (reserved seat on the shinkansen), rounded. Check them before you travel.
